## 0. Preamble (verbatim from the submission)

EXT-24 is the programme’s closure and transition-to-operations CR. It should not simply declare the catalogue complete because EXT-23 validation has been implemented. It must establish that the entire enrichment, reconciliation and conformance programme has produced an auditable baseline, that residual gaps are explicitly governed, and that ownership has transferred into a sustainable continuous-improvement lifecycle.

I recommend defining EXT-24 around three outcomes:

* Formal closure: demonstrate what was completed, what remains open and what has been accepted as an exception.
* Operational governance: establish ownership, review cycles, change control, evidence refresh and conformance monitoring.
* Continuous evolution: ensure future changes to the process catalogue, metamodel, related catalogues and organisational practices do not silently degrade its integrity.

1. Executive Summary

This Change Request establishes the formal closure of the Business Process Operational Documentation Extension programme and transitions the Business Process catalogue into a continuously governed operational asset.

EXT-08 through EXT-23 collectively establish the foundations required to develop, enrich, reconcile and validate the catalogue.

However, completing the individual CRs does not automatically establish that the resulting catalogue can be maintained sustainably.

Without a formal transition into operational governance, the catalogue risks gradually losing the quality achieved through the programme.

Potential causes include:

* Organisational changes that invalidate process responsibilities.
* Changes to business processes that are not reflected in the catalogue.
* New or modified capabilities that introduce new process requirements.
* Changes to Information Systems and Information Systems Functions.
* Changes to policies, controls, risks and regulatory requirements.
* Changes to metrics and performance expectations.
* New AI agents and automation capabilities that alter how activities and tasks are performed.
* Changes to the OpenDEA metamodel.
* Schema and vocabulary evolution.
* Stale or superseded evidence.
* Documentation that no longer reflects canonical entity data.
* New contributors introducing inconsistent modelling practices.
* Exceptions and deferred remediation items remaining unresolved.

EXT-24 establishes the governance framework that prevents these conditions from silently degrading the catalogue.

It introduces:

1. Formal programme closure.
2. An authoritative catalogue baseline.
3. Operational ownership and accountability.
4. Entity lifecycle governance.
5. Periodic review and evidence refresh.
6. Continuous conformance monitoring.
7. Change-impact management.
8. Cross-catalogue dependency governance.
9. Schema, profile and vocabulary governance.
10. Quality measurement and reporting.
11. Exception and remediation management.
12. Continuous improvement and future CR management.

The fundamental transition is:

From periodic catalogue enrichment as a project to continuous catalogue stewardship as an organisational capability.

EXT-24 is therefore both a closure CR and the establishment of an ongoing governance operating model.

3. Problem Statement

A catalogue is not complete simply because its current entities have been enriched.

It is complete only to the extent that its current scope, structure, content and limitations are understood, and that an effective mechanism exists to maintain them.

The programme must therefore address two distinct concerns.

3.1 Programme completion

The programme needs an authoritative answer to:

* Which entities were assessed?
* Which entities were enriched?
* Which entities were reconciled?
* Which requirements were implemented?
* Which conformance checks are operational?
* Which gaps remain?
* Which exceptions were approved?
* Which items were deferred?
* Which deliverables were accepted?
* What constitutes the final programme baseline?

3.2 Sustainable operational governance

The catalogue needs an ongoing mechanism to determine:

* When an entity must be reviewed.
* Who is responsible for maintaining it.
* Which organisational changes require updates.
* Which evidence has become stale.
* Which relationships need revalidation.
* How changes to the metamodel affect the catalogue.
* How cross-catalogue dependencies are maintained.
* How new modelling requirements are introduced.
* How non-conformance is detected and resolved.
* How quality improvements are prioritised.

EXT-24 addresses both concerns.

5.2 Out of scope

EXT-24 does not:

* Reopen all completed enrichment work without an identified reason.
* Introduce another round of indiscriminate catalogue backfill.
* Replace the established metamodel.
* Redefine L0-L4 semantics.
* Make every entity subject to identical review frequencies.
* Require all evidence to be publicly stored in the repository.
* Assume that all process information can be fully automated.
* Replace enterprise process ownership or operational governance.
* Automatically certify the effectiveness of documented processes.
* Automatically approve changes to process semantics.
* Introduce new relationship types without appropriate metamodel governance.

Where new substantive requirements are discovered, they should be managed through separate CRs or approved changes to the relevant architectural artefacts.

6. Programme Closure Framework

REQ-24-01  -  Formal programme completion assessment

The implementation shall produce a formal assessment of the completion status of EXT-08 through EXT-23.

For each CR, the assessment shall identify:

* CR identifier.
* Approved scope.
* Requirements.
* Deliverables.
* Implementation status.
* Acceptance criteria status.
* Validation evidence.
* Outstanding issues.
* Approved deviations.
* Deferred requirements.
* Dependencies.
* Closure recommendation.

The assessment shall distinguish:

* completed
* completed_with_approved_exceptions
* partially_completed
* deferred
* blocked
* not_started
* not_applicable

A CR must not be marked completed solely because its document has been produced.

Completion requires evidence that its approved implementation and acceptance requirements have been satisfied.

REQ-24-02  -  Requirement closure traceability

The programme shall maintain traceability from the original requirements to their implementation and validation evidence.

The traceability chain shall be:

CR → Requirement → Design Decision → Implementation → Test or Review Evidence → Acceptance Decision

Where an ADR or implementation artefact is not applicable, that status must be explicit.

The programme must identify:

* Requirements implemented as specified.
* Requirements implemented with approved variations.
* Requirements partially implemented.
* Requirements not implemented.
* Requirements superseded by later decisions.
* Requirements deferred to future work.

Programme closure does not require every conceivable future improvement to have been implemented.

It requires a defensible baseline and an effective governance mechanism for managing what remains.

8. Operational Governance Model

REQ-24-09  -  Establish governance roles

The implementation shall define the roles required to govern the catalogue.

The following are proposed governance responsibilities. They should be mapped to actual organisational roles or existing repository governance conventions rather than treated as mandatory new organisational positions.

Governance role	Primary responsibility
Catalogue Steward	Maintains catalogue integrity, consistency and governance practices.
Business Process Owner	Confirms the meaning, scope and operational accuracy of relevant Business Processes.
Process Domain Reviewer	Reviews process models within an assigned domain.
Entity Maintainer	Updates canonical entity information and documentation.
Metamodel Steward	Ensures alignment with OpenDEA metamodel semantics and evolution.
Data and Evidence Reviewer	Assesses evidence quality, provenance and verification.
Conformance Maintainer	Maintains schemas, profiles, rules and validation tooling.
Repository Maintainer	Maintains repository integrity, CI and contribution controls.
Governance Authority	Approves significant changes, exceptions and policy decisions.

A single individual may fulfil multiple roles where appropriate.

The model must distinguish the technical responsibility for maintaining an entity from the business authority to confirm its operational meaning.

REQ-24-10  -  Ownership assignment

Every in-scope L2 Business Process shall have an identifiable ownership disposition.

Where an actual owner is known, the canonical entity or approved governance record should reference that owner using the appropriate existing model.

Where ownership cannot be established, the entity shall be explicitly marked as awaiting owner confirmation or otherwise dispositioned.

L3 Activities and L4 Tasks may have distinct responsible parties, performers or authorities where justified.

Ownership must not be automatically inherited from the parent entity.

REQ-24-11  -  Governance authority and decision rights

* Exception approval.
* Schema evolution.
* Profile changes.
* Vocabulary changes.
* Validation rule changes.
* Cross-catalogue relationship changes.

Decision rights must be explicit and consistent with the organisation’s actual authority structure.

REQ-24-12  -  Governance operating procedures

9. Process Entity Lifecycle Governance

REQ-24-13  -  Define the entity lifecycle

The catalogue shall support a controlled lifecycle for process entities.

The proposed conceptual lifecycle is:

proposed → under_review → approved → active → under_review / superseded / deprecated / retired

The implementation shall reconcile this lifecycle with the actual entity status vocabulary already established in the repository.

The lifecycle must distinguish entity approval from the operational status of the real-world process.

For example, a process entity may be approved as a catalogue representation while the underlying business process is under redesign.

These are separate facts and must not be conflated.

REQ-24-14  -  New entity governance

New entities shall follow an established contribution and approval process.

The process shall include:

1. Proposal.
2. Entity classification.
3. Parent assignment.
4. Scope and purpose definition.
5. Applicable decomposition profile.
6. Operational documentation.
7. Evidence and provenance.
8. Relationship validation.
9. Review and approval.
10. Canonical publication.
11. Documentation generation.
12. Conformance validation.

New entities must not be added as unstructured documentation outside the canonical entity architecture.

REQ-24-15  -  Entity modification governance

Material changes shall be traceable.

Examples include:

* Purpose changes.
* Boundary changes.
* Responsibility changes.
* Input and output changes.
* Transformation changes.
* Parent-child reassignment.
* Changes to rules and controls.
* Changes to dependencies.
* Changes to system interfaces.
* Changes to evidence and verification status.

Changes must identify the reason and relevant authority.

REQ-24-16  -  Entity deprecation and supersession

Entities that are no longer valid shall not be silently deleted where doing so would destroy traceability.

The lifecycle mechanism shall support:

* Deprecation.
* Supersession.
* Replacement references.
* Historical traceability.
* Relationship migration.
* Documentation updates.

The implementation must distinguish a process that no longer exists from a process that has been renamed, reorganised or replaced.

REQ-24-17  -  Parent-child lifecycle coordination

Changes to a parent entity shall trigger an impact assessment of affected children.

Examples:

* L2 Process boundary change triggers L3 Activity review.
* L3 Activity boundary change triggers L4 Task review.
* L1 Process Group changes trigger L2 membership review.
* L0 Process Scope changes trigger L1 and associated L2 scope review.

Changes to a child shall trigger a review of its immediate parent where the child’s contribution or coverage may have changed.

The impact assessment must not automatically rewrite child entities.

10. Periodic Review and Evidence Refresh

REQ-24-18  -  Establish review cycles

The implementation shall establish periodic reviews appropriate to entity significance, change exposure and evidence requirements.

The review model shall not impose identical frequencies on all entities.

The following are proposed starting points, subject to governance approval:

Entity or artefact	Suggested review interval
L0 Process Scope	Annually and on material scope changes
L1 Process Group	Annually and on membership changes
L2 Business Process	At least annually, or more frequently for high-impact processes
L3 Activity	Every 12-24 months or when its parent changes materially
L4 Task	Every 12-24 months or when its Activity, systems or operational method changes
Evidence references	According to source volatility and evidence classification
Schemas and profiles	On approved model changes and scheduled governance review
Validation rules	On requirement changes and periodic rule effectiveness review
Exceptions and deferrals	At their approved review or expiry date

These intervals are policy defaults, not automatic claims that an entity remains accurate until its next review.

REQ-24-19  -  Risk-based review prioritisation

Review frequency shall be influenced by:

* Business criticality.
* Regulatory significance.
* Operational risk.
* Rate of organisational change.
* System dependency.
* Evidence volatility.
* Degree of automation.
* AI involvement.
* Previous non-conformance.
* Historical frequency of material changes.

Where appropriate, the catalogue should use existing risk, control or criticality references rather than introducing duplicate scoring systems.

REQ-24-20  -  Evidence freshness management

Evidence shall be reviewed for continuing relevance.

The framework shall identify:

* Expired or outdated sources.
* Superseded documents.
* Broken source references.
* Evidence associated with discontinued systems.
* Evidence that no longer supports the current assertion.
* Conflicting new evidence.
* Evidence whose authority has changed.

The age of evidence alone must not automatically invalidate it.

Freshness must be assessed in relation to source type, assertion type and the nature of the change.

REQ-24-21  -  Evidence re-verification

Material operational assertions shall be reverified when significant changes affect their underlying assumptions.

The process shall record:

* Assertion or entity.
* Previous evidence.
* New evidence.
* Verification outcome.
* Reviewer.
* Review date.
* Resulting entity changes.

The framework must preserve historical provenance rather than replacing old evidence without traceability.

REQ-24-22  -  Overdue review management

The governance framework shall identify overdue reviews.

Overdue status must be visible in reporting.

It shall not automatically mean that the entity is false or invalid.

The system should distinguish:

* Review due.
* Review overdue.
* Review completed.
* Review deferred with approval.
* Review blocked by missing evidence.
* Review not applicable.

11. Event-Triggered Governance

Periodic reviews alone are insufficient because important changes may occur immediately after a review.

The implementation shall define events that initiate an impact assessment or review.

Examples include:

Organisational events

* Organisational restructuring.
* Changes in business ownership.
* Changes in responsibility or accountability.
* New business units.
* Mergers or acquisitions.
* Outsourcing or insourcing.

Business events

* New products or services.
* Changes in customer journeys.
* New business capabilities.
* New business functions.
* Changes to operating models.
* Introduction or retirement of business processes.

Technology events

* Information System introduction.
* Information System replacement.
* Information System retirement.
* Changes to Information Systems Functions.
* Major integrations.
* Introduction of automation.
* Introduction of AI agents.
* Changes to agent responsibilities or permissions.

Governance events

* New policies.
* Regulatory changes.
* Risk assessments.
* Audit findings.
* Control failures.
* Material incidents.
* Changes to organisational governance.

Architecture events

* Metamodel changes.
* Entity schema changes.
* Controlled vocabulary changes.
* Relationship model changes.

Evidence events

* New authoritative documentation.
* Contradictory operational evidence.
* Expired evidence.
* Process owner confirmation.

REQ-24-24  -  Event-to-entity impact mapping

The implementation shall support identifying affected process entities when a triggering event occurs.

For example:

Triggering event	Potentially affected entities
Business Capability change	Referenced L2 Processes and associated L3 Activities
Business Function change	Processes and Activities linked to that function
Information System replacement	Processes, Activities and Tasks with relevant system dependencies
IS Function change	Processes and Tasks that depend on the affected function
Policy change	Referenced processes, applicable rules and controls
Metric definition change	Processes using the affected Metric
AI agent introduction	Activities and Tasks with changed performers or responsibilities
L2 boundary change	All affected L3 Activities and their L4 descendants
Metamodel relationship change	Entities using the affected relationship

The impact mechanism shall use existing canonical references wherever possible.

It must not depend exclusively on textual similarity.

REQ-24-25  -  Change-triggered reconciliation

Where a material change affects decomposition, the relevant EXT-22 reconciliation requirements shall be repeated.

For example:

* Adding an Activity requires parent contribution and coverage review.
* Removing an Activity requires reassessment of remaining process coverage.
* Reassigning a Task requires review of both affected Activities.
* Changing a Business Process boundary requires review of its Activities and relevant interfaces.

A full catalogue-wide reconciliation is not required for every change if the impact can be safely bounded.

However, the framework must provide an escalation mechanism when local changes affect broader catalogue integrity.

12. Cross-Catalogue Governance

REQ-24-26  -  Establish cross-catalogue dependency governance

The Business Process catalogue shall maintain its relationships with relevant OpenDEA catalogues.

These may include:

* Business Capabilities.
* Business Functions.
* Information Systems.
* Information Systems Functions.
* Metrics.
* Organisational entities.
* Policies.
* Risks.
* Controls.
* Agent profiles.
* Agent skills.
* Value Streams.

The actual scope shall be determined by the existing metamodel and repository ecosystem.

REQ-24-27  -  Canonical reference maintenance

Cross-catalogue references shall use canonical identifiers and approved relationship types.

The governance framework shall identify:

* Deleted or deprecated references.
* Renamed entities.
* Superseded entities.
* Broken relationships.
* Relationship type changes.
* Unresolved references.
* Conflicting definitions.
* External catalogue changes.

The Business Process catalogue must not create local copies of external entity semantics merely to avoid maintaining references.

REQ-24-28  -  Cross-catalogue change notifications

Where supported by the wider OpenDEA repository architecture, changes to referenced entities should trigger notifications or validation.

For example, a change to an Information System Function may require review of affected Tasks.

A change to a Business Capability may require reassessment of its associated process coverage.

The implementation should use repository events, dependency manifests or existing integration mechanisms where available.

REQ-24-29  -  Cross-catalogue ownership boundaries

The Business Process catalogue shall own process-specific information.

Other catalogues shall remain authoritative for their respective concepts.

Examples:

* Business Process purpose and transformation belong to the process model.
* Business Capability definition belongs to the capability catalogue.
* Information System identity belongs to the Information Systems catalogue.
* Metric definition belongs to the Metric catalogue.
* Risk definition belongs to the Risk catalogue.
* Control definition belongs to the applicable control model.

Cross-catalogue references must not create competing definitions of these concepts.

13. Metamodel and Architectural Evolution Governance

The catalogue shall remain aligned with the OpenDEA metamodel.

Changes to the metamodel that affect:

* Entity definitions.
* Attribute semantics.
* Relationship types.
* Relationship cardinality.

* Entity lifecycle.

shall trigger an impact assessment.

REQ-24-31  -  Schema evolution governance

Schema changes shall follow controlled change management.

Every material schema change must include:

* Change rationale.
* Affected entity types.
* Compatibility assessment.
* Migration strategy.
* Validation changes.
* Regression tests.
* Documentation updates.
* Approval.

REQ-24-32  -  Profile and vocabulary governance

Changes to decomposition profiles and controlled vocabularies must be approved and versioned.

The implementation shall identify affected entities and assess whether migration is required.

A vocabulary value must not be removed while existing canonical entities still depend on it unless an approved migration or compatibility mechanism exists.

REQ-24-33  -  Validation rule governance

Validation rules established under EXT-23 shall be maintained as governed architectural artefacts.

The framework shall support:

* New rules.
* Rule modification.
* Rule deprecation.
* Rule replacement.
* Rule severity changes.
* Rule applicability changes.
* Regression testing.
* Historical interpretation.

A rule change must not silently invalidate previously approved exceptions or governance decisions.

REQ-24-34  -  Architectural decision records

Material architectural changes shall be governed through ADRs where appropriate.

The catalogue governance process shall distinguish:

* A change to an individual process entity.
* A change to the entity schema.
* A change to a decomposition profile.
* A change to the metamodel.
* A change to repository implementation.
* A change to governance policy.

The appropriate change mechanism must be selected before implementation.

REQ-24-40  -  Improvement classification

Improvement items shall be classified.

Category	Description
Defect correction	Corrects incorrect or invalid catalogue information.
Completeness improvement	Fills an identified information or documentation gap.
Semantic improvement	Improves conceptual clarity or modelling quality.
Structural improvement	Corrects entity hierarchy or relationships.
Metamodel alignment	Aligns the catalogue with architectural evolution.
Automation improvement	Improves tooling and validation.
Governance improvement	Improves accountability and control.
Integration improvement	Improves cross-catalogue traceability.
Innovation	Supports new business, technology or AI-related modelling needs.

REQ-24-41  -  CR initiation

Material improvements shall be managed through formal Change Requests.

The governance process shall determine whether an issue requires:

* Direct entity correction.
* Documentation update.
* New ADR.
* Schema change.
* Profile change.
* Vocabulary change.
* Validation rule change.
* Metamodel CR.
* New catalogue or entity type.
* Cross-catalogue integration change.

A change must not be implemented as an informal data correction when it materially changes the architecture or semantics.

REQ-24-42  -  Continuous improvement prioritisation

Improvements shall be prioritised according to:

* Business impact.
* Risk reduction.
* Regulatory significance.
* Catalogue quality.
* Operational value.
* Architectural alignment.
* Dependency impact.
* Implementation effort.
* Urgency.

The framework should avoid prioritising cosmetic documentation changes over material structural or semantic defects.

16. AI and Automation Governance

The Business Process catalogue is expected to evolve alongside increasing automation and agentic capabilities.

EXT-24 shall establish governance for these changes without prematurely changing the process decomposition model.

REQ-24-43  -  AI-assisted catalogue maintenance

AI tools may support:

* Entity discovery.
* Documentation drafting.
* Evidence classification.
* Potential duplicate detection.
* Parent-child contribution analysis.
* Decomposition gap detection.
* Cross-catalogue reference discovery.
* Semantic consistency review.
* Documentation generation.
* Conformance issue explanation.

AI-generated recommendations must remain distinguishable from verified catalogue information.

REQ-24-44  -  Human verification

AI-assisted changes shall follow EXT-15 evidence and EXT-16 authoring requirements.

AI systems must not independently:

* Approve Business Process semantics.
* Confirm actual operational practice.
* Assign organisational accountability.
* Authorise entity deprecation.
* Approve exceptions.
* Change metamodel semantics.
* Declare a catalogue fully reconciled.

Human approval shall remain mandatory for decisions requiring business authority.

REQ-24-45  -  Agentic and automated work representation

Where an Activity or Task is performed or supported by an AI agent or automation system, the process catalogue shall represent the business responsibility and operational work according to the established model.

References to agents and systems shall use the relevant canonical catalogues.

The process catalogue must distinguish:

* Business responsibility.
* Human performer.
* AI agent performer.
* Information System.
* Information Systems Function.
* Automation capability.
* Accountability and decision authority.

An AI agent’s ability to execute a Task does not automatically make it accountable for the Business Process.

REQ-24-46  -  Automation-induced process change

Where automation materially changes how work is performed, the affected process entities shall be reviewed.

The review shall determine whether the change affects:

* Activity purpose.
* Task boundaries.
* Inputs.
* Outputs.
* Transformation.
* Responsibilities.
* Accountability.
* Controls.
* Evidence.
* Dependencies.
* Metrics.

The introduction of automation must not automatically result in a new Task or Activity.

The decision must be based on whether the underlying business responsibility or functional composition has changed.

18. Governance Operating Cycle

The ongoing governance cycle shall follow this conceptual sequence:

1. Monitor

Observe conformance, evidence freshness, entity lifecycle and cross-catalogue changes.

2. Detect

Identify changes, defects, outdated assertions, broken references and emerging requirements.

3. Assess

Determine business impact, affected entities, evidence requirements and governance implications.

4. Decide

Approve corrections, changes, exceptions, deferrals or architectural improvements.

5. Implement

Update canonical entities, documentation, relationships, schemas or tooling as appropriate.

6. Validate

Run EXT-23 conformance checks and any required EXT-22 reconciliation.

7. Approve

Obtain the required business, architectural and governance approvals.

8. Publish

Release approved catalogue changes and associated documentation.

9. Review

Monitor the outcome and update the improvement backlog.

This is a governance lifecycle, not an instruction to model every process as a workflow.

27. My recommendation: one final consolidation exercise

I would not immediately begin another independent enrichment sequence. The more valuable next step is to consolidate EXT-08 through EXT-24 into a formal Business Process Operational Documentation and Governance Specification v1.0.

That specification should contain five integrated parts:

* Normative model: canonical entity semantics, L0-L4 decomposition rules, relationships and profiles.
* Operational data model: schema, evidence, provenance, completeness, responsibilities and controlled vocabularies.
* Engineering specification: authoring, generation, validation, CI, reconciliation and reporting.
* Governance specification: ownership, lifecycle, approvals, exceptions, review cycles and continuous improvement.
* Implementation and acceptance baseline: CR traceability, ADR decisions, migration status, test coverage and operational readiness.

One important qualification: the CRs produced in this conversation are proposed specifications. Their implementation status, repository alignment and acceptance must still be verified against the actual repository before the specification can be declared an implemented v1.0.

This consolidation would give the OpenDEA Business Process catalogue a single coherent architectural and operational reference rather than requiring contributors to interpret seventeen separate CRs independently.
